## The Atelier comes with the game

**The bench that builds the buildings is now yours.** There is an ATELIER button
on the title screen, and it opens the same tool the game's own longhouses were
drawn in — walls and roofs and doors on a lattice where every dimension is a
sixteenth of a metre, furniture, a colour for every piece, and a save that the
village can raise. It takes over from the game rather than sitting beside it:
you draw a building, and you carry it in afterwards.

**Villagers and animals stop walking through walls.** The path a walker takes
knew about water and cliffs and nothing anybody had built, so the shortest way
across a village went straight through the hall. Buildings are solid now, for
people and creatures alike, with one rule for both: you may enter the building
you are going to, and leave the one you are in, and never cut through one you are
merely passing.

**A door for two.** Halls and barns can carry a double door, and two people can
use it at once — each leaf is its own way through, so nobody queues behind
somebody else's shoulder.

## Under the hood

- Roofs and gables carry their own pitch. Every roof in the world stood at thirty
  degrees because there was one number for all of them; a maker can pull a ridge
  now, and a gable follows the roof it stands under.
- Beams can be cut at the angle of the roof they meet, rather than stopping
  square and standing off it. The bench and the game learned a new shape between
  them to do it — a mitre, which is what a saw leaves.
- The smoke behind the title stopped flickering, and sits behind the lettering
  rather than below it. It had been trading its colour with the fade that brings
  the title up, once a frame.
- A building is drawn in stages now: what stands after the footings, after the
  frame, after the walls — each one its own complete picture, so the village
  raises what the maker drew rather than what the game guessed.
- Said plainly, because it is still true: rivers step where two courses meet at
  different levels, and the near ground is still visibly sharper than the planet
  it sits on. Both are next.
