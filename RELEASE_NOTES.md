## Mornings have mist in them

Ground fog, lying in the low ground of damp and cold country and burning off
as the sun gets up. It is marched through rather than washed over the picture,
so it fills the bottom of a valley and leaves the ridge standing out of it —
and looking along a hollow shows you more of it than looking across one.

- **It belongs to particular country.** Damp air and cold air carry it, and it
  pools where the ground lies low compared to what is around it. Most of the
  world is clear; a wet hollow at dawn is not.
- **It comes in banks** rather than as one flat sheet — thick along one hollow,
  thinning, giving out, picking up again further on.
- **A day is not a mirror.** It gathers through the small hours, stands
  thickest at dawn, burns off over a morning, and comes back late and thin in
  the evening — never to the morning's depth.
- **It is the sky, seen edge-on**, so it goes warm at dawn on its own. Look
  across a valley into a low sun and it blows out bright; turn around and it
  is cool and quiet.

## Nothing stands lit in country you have not walked

The veil over unknown land used to be a property of the ground and the things
growing on it, which meant everything else was missed — villagers, animals and
buildings all walked about lit in the dark. An explorer out past the frontier
was a lit explorer; a wolf out there was a lit wolf.

Now it covers everything that is drawn at all, which includes anything ever
added later.

## Mountains come to a point

They were flat on top, held lakes up there, and were miserable to cross.

- **Summits are summits.** A range used to be a broad table with a hundred
  meters of camber on it; now the ridgelines carry the height and the peaks
  come to a point.
- **The water runs off them.** Roughly half as much standing water above the
  treeline, and what is left is a real mountain lake rather than a tarn in
  every dip.
- **There are ways through.** Peaks stand apart with valleys running between
  them, and a valley between two peaks is a pass.

Taller, too, and half again as steep.

## The wilderness gets its own creatures

- **Camels** in the desert — all legs and neck, and taller than you are.
- **Bears** in the woods and the north. One bear in a wood is worth six of
  anything else.
- **Polar bears** on the northern ice, and they are the biggest thing that
  walks: taller at the shoulder than a villager is tall.
- **Penguins** in the far south — and the two never meet, because the north
  and the south of this world get different animals, the way they do here.
- **Goblins**, green and short and in camps of four to seven, out past the
  ground your village knows. They hunt.
