## The god descends

**The front door is the world** — the studio's mark fades over black (any key skips it), and then: no menu wall. The title's gold lettering hangs over your living, breathing world — villagers about their morning, smoke curling behind the logotype, the whole valley drifting past through a dreaming lens. Press Begin and there is no loading screen: the veil thins, the smoke blows away, and the camera dives from the god's height down to the village banner. You were always up there; you simply came down.

**Exit to Title** — Escape now offers the road back up. The world you leave is swept away and a brand-new one is founded behind the returning veil — new land, new founders, new god, morning light — so Begin from the title is always a true new game. Loading a save still returns a world exactly as it was.

**The People window, finished** — the codex's first room built to the mockup: a roster of painted busts, the portrait standing in a carved charcoal niche, the eleven measures of a soul in engraved capitals, wants and wonders and the latest doings dated in the margin — and below, a life story banded by day, kin you can click through, craft bars that fill as skill grows. Cinzel and Garamond, charcoal and gold, square steel corners: the standard every panel will be brought to, one by one.

**THE CHRONICLE, rebound** — the world's history reads like a book: day bands, each ledger inked in its own colour — lives in red, works in timber, faith in gold, the world in green — small glyphs for people, houses, sparks and trees, repeats folded into ×N, and a census standing at the foot of the page.

**A working life accumulates craft** — villagers grow skilled at what they do, green hands to master, and it shows: practiced fishers land heavier catches, veteran miners cut faster, seasoned healers save more of the sick. The planners stop churning souls between trades — anyone fresh to a craft gets three days' grace before they can be moved — which also ends the tragedy of the village eating its own carpenter and stalling every half-built house.

**Every tree is a tree, every rock is a rock** — nothing is painted scenery anymore. Cut a tree and that very tree topples with a crash and is carried home; the forest thins in place around its stump. Mine out a boulder and it sinks spent into the earth. The renderer never notices the difference — and neither does your framerate.

**Paths form like paths** — a commute becomes a track becomes a road, twice as quickly as before.

**Mercy for mothers** — one couple managed twenty-three children in their first year. Nursing now spaces births the way the world intended.

**Windows behave** — the active window rides to the front like a proper desktop, clicks no longer fall through panels onto the toolbar, the close button is a bare saltire, and the divine hand holds rock-steady over buttons and moving cameras alike.

**The game wears its mark** — Baz's gold logotype on the title screen, and the stone cairn medallion on the Dock, the taskbar, and the launcher shelf.
